Cell Biology
Cell Structure
Cells are the basic units of life.

Animal Cells
Animal cells contain:

Nucleus
Cytoplasm
Cell membrane
Mitochondria
Ribosomes
Plant Cells
Plant cells contain all the structures found in animal cells plus:

Cell wall
Chloroplasts
Permanent vacuole
Function of Cell Structures
Nucleus
Contains genetic material (DNA) and controls cell activities.

Cytoplasm
Site of most chemical reactions.

Cell Membrane
Controls movement of substances into and out of the cell.

Mitochondria
Site of aerobic respiration.

Ribosomes
Site of protein synthesis.

Cell Wall
Made from cellulose and strengthens plant cells.

Chloroplasts
Contain chlorophyll for photosynthesis.

Vacuole
Contains cell sap and helps support the plant.

Prokaryotic Cells
Bacteria are prokaryotes.

They contain:

Cytoplasm
Cell membrane
Cell wall
Circular DNA
Plasmids
Ribosomes
They do not contain a nucleus.

Transport in Cells
Diffusion
Diffusion is the net movement of particles from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration.

Examples:

Oxygen entering blood from lungs
Carbon dioxide leaving blood
Factors affecting diffusion:

Concentration gradient
Temperature
Surface area
Diffusion distance
Osmosis
Osmosis is the movement of water molecules through a partially permeable membrane from a dilute solution to a concentrated solution.

In a Dilute Solution
Water enters the cell.

In a Concentrated Solution
Water leaves the cell.

Active Transport
Active transport moves substances against a concentration gradient using energy released by respiration.

Examples:

Root hair cells absorbing minerals
Small intestine absorbing glucose

Enzymes
Enzymes are biological catalysts.

They speed up reactions without being used up.

Breaks starch into sugars.

Protease
Breaks proteins into amino acids.

Lipase
Breaks lipids into fatty acids and glycerol.
