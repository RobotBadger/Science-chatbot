Atoms are the smallest particles that can exist as elements.

An atom contains:

Protons
Neutrons
Electrons

Atomic Number
The atomic number is the number of protons inside the nucleus.

Example:

Carbon:

Atomic number = 6
Protons = 6
Electrons = 6
Mass Number
Mass Number = Protons + Neutrons

Example:

Carbon-12:

Protons = 6
Neutrons = 6
Mass Number = 12

Different number of neutrons
Examples:

Carbon-14

Medical tracers

Development of the Atomic Model
Dalton
Atoms were solid spheres.

Thomson
Discovered electrons.

Proposed the plum pudding model.

Rutherford
Discovered the nucleus.

Proposed the nuclear model.

Bohr
Suggested electrons occupy fixed energy levels.

Chadwick
Discovered neutrons.

The Periodic Table
Mendeleev
Mendeleev arranged elements by atomic mass and left gaps for undiscovered elements.

Modern Periodic Table
Elements are arranged by atomic number.

Periods
Periods run horizontally.

The period number equals the number of occupied electron shells.

Groups
Groups run vertically.

Elements in the same group have similar properties.

Group 1: Alkali Metals
Elements:

Lithium
Sodium
Potassium
Properties:

Soft
Low density
Highly reactive
Reaction with water:

Metal + Water → Metal Hydroxide + Hydrogen

Reactivity increases down the group.

Group 7: Halogens
Elements:

Fluorine
Chlorine
Bromine
Iodine
Properties:

Non-metals
Toxic
Coloured vapours
Reactivity decreases down the group.

More reactive halogens displace less reactive halogens from compounds.

Elements:

Bonding
Ionic Bonding
Ionic bonding involves transfer of electrons.

Example:

Sodium + Chlorine

Sodium loses one electron.

Chlorine gains one electron.

Produces:

Na+
Cl-
Opposite charges attract.

Properties of Ionic Compounds
High melting points
Conduct electricity when molten or dissolved
Usually form crystals

Quantitative Chemistry
Conservation of Mass
Mass is never created or destroyed.

Total mass of reactants equals total mass of products.

Relative Formula Mass
Mr = Sum of atomic masses.

Example:

CO₂

Carbon = 12

Oxygen = 16

Mr = 12 + 16 + 16

Mr = 44

Moles
One mole contains:

6.02 × 10²³ particles

Formula
Moles = Mass ÷ Mr

Energy Changes
Exothermic Reactions
Transfer energy to surroundings.

Temperature rises.

Examples:

Combustion
Neutralisation
Endothermic Reactions
Take in energy from surroundings.

Temperature falls.

Examples:

Thermal decomposition
Photosynthesis
Rates of Reaction
Collision Theory
Reactions occur when particles collide successfully.

Successful collisions require:

Sufficient energy
Correct orientation
Factors Affecting Rate
Temperature
Higher temperature increases rate.

Concentration
Higher concentration increases rate.

Pressure
Higher pressure increases rate.

Surface Area
Greater surface area increases rate.

Catalysts
Lower activation energy.

Catalysts are not used up.

Reversible Reactions
A reversible reaction can proceed in both directions.

Example:

Hydrated Copper Sulfate ⇌ Anhydrous Copper Sulfate + Water

Dynamic Equilibrium
At equilibrium:

Forward reaction continues
Reverse reaction continues
Rates are equal
